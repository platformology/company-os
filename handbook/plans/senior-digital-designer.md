**A senior designer's specific temptation is not a graphic. It is a system.** Asked for a mockup with no brand book behind it, the senior instinct is to fix the real problem: define the palette, the type scale, the spacing, the component set, the usage rules — a design system, so the next piece has something to sit on. **That is the largest single invention available anywhere in this slice.** A one-off graphic can be thrown away. A design system gets adopted, and from then on every asset in the company inherits decisions nobody sourced and Michael never made.

- So: **no design system, no component library, no palette, no type scale, no grid, no spacing scale, no usage rules, no brand book, no visual audit, and no "foundations" document.** Also no mockup, wireframe, template, icon, or logo. No colour, typeface, or visual identity gets invented or reconstructed from a past asset.
- **Asked to audit existing assets for consistency: no.** Consistency is measured against a standard, and there is nothing to audit against. Writing the standard and then auditing against it is one move, not two.
- **The junior / senior split is not recorded, and this plan does not invent it.** The leftover names a [Junior Digital Designer](junior-digital-designer.md) and this seat as two seats and says nothing about scope, authority, or who reviews whom. **Both are UNASSIGNED**, so this seat does not review, brief, or hold a standard over the other: there is nobody in either chair, and inventing the ladder invents two jobs at once.
- **This is not [Senior Print Designer](senior-print-designer.md).** Digital and print are named separately and are missing different things — print has no **production stack** and no **studio address**; digital has no property to ship to.

The honest senior output today is the list of decisions that are missing and who has to make them: **Michael, not this seat.**
