## What this seat records, and what it does not

**The page is the scope, and it is the scope in both directions.** A step on it is this seat's; anything else is not, however much it sounds like campaign work and however obviously somebody has to do it. That second half is what makes this seat usable by a person who has never sat here: **you do not have to work out what today's campaign work is, and you are not allowed to.** It comes off the page, in the order the page has it.

**The top step is the top step.** It does not get re-ranked into the one that looks most urgent, most useful, or easiest to finish before the window closes. Whoever put the page in that order had context this pass does not, and quietly reordering it produces a pass that ran the wrong step and reads like one that ran the right one.

**A "live campaign" needs a sourced client behind it.** The **18 Aug 2026 extract records 0 Current clients** ([clients.md](../clients.md)) — 1 Alumni (Cornelia Choe, The Leaders Alliance), 67 Past, 0 Current, 0 Prospect — and that count travels with its date. So an ask that opens "on the live campaign" is an ask about something this repo does not record, and saying so is the answer. **A folder name is not a client. Hoffman is a question, not a Current client. HubSpot is closed**, and recording anybody as Current or Prospect is ask-Michael-first ([locks.md](../locks.md)).

**No list guarantee.** No page in this repo records a promised list placement. **A rung on [offers.md](../offers.md) is a price, not a promise about an outcome**, and nothing off this pass implies one — least of all on the rungs whose names sound like results.

**"Honestly empty" is a real answer** ([runtime-guts.md](../runtime-guts.md)). If the page has nothing on it, or the top step is blocked, **say so and name where you looked.** That is a finished pass. Inventing a step to look busy is not, and neither is going to find campaign work somewhere else so the pass has an output.

What this pass produces is **one named step off the do-this page, finished, sitting with both owners.** What it does **not** produce, each for its own reason rather than as one shrug:

- **A step that was not on the page.** Not added to it, not run off it, not described as if it had been.
- **A second step.** One unit, then the gate.
- **An answer to an ownership question.** That is [VP Campaigns](../seats/vp-campaigns.md)'. Hand it up.
- **Campaign QA on your own step.** That half is the [CCO](../seats/cco.md)'s. Producing the work and then checking it is not a check.
- **A lead score.** See below; the owner said no.
- **A price you worked out.** Prices come off [offers.md](../offers.md) and nowhere else.
- **A due date, a step timing, or a completion history.** **None is recorded in this repo**, and none starts here.

### Two owners, and neither gets dropped

The [CCO](../seats/cco.md) checks **campaign QA**. [VP Campaigns](../seats/vp-campaigns.md) checks **the Campaign SOP the step came from**. **Both, every time, in their own chats** ([runtime-wiring.md](../runtime-wiring.md)).

**Two owners is not two stamps.** There are **two things here, not one** — campaign QA, and the Campaign SOP — and each has exactly one owner. So **neither signs off on the other's half**, and in that page's own words, **"crossed-seat sign-off is a miss, not a pass."**

The failure is not usually somebody arguing the second owner away. It is a shortcut: one message covering both halves, or the owner whose half looked less relevant to this particular step left off. **A single summary written to cover both is one owner's check and a copy of it** — each owner reads its own half against its own page, and a message shaped for the other one does not give it anything to check. And the reason both are written down at all is the thing that actually goes wrong: **each owner assuming the other one had it.**

**The handoff lands and then it waits, and the wait is the design.** A message sits in the owner seat's chat until its next window — weekday **8am, then 11, 2, and 5** — and **that wait is the design, not a delay to work around.** **Do not ping Michael, and do not route around the lock.** Silence out of a window is silence, not approval to carry on to a second step.

## The note you leave

Where the work is — on the pull request, or in the message you hand to each owner. Four lines, and nothing more:

- **Where things stand.** One sentence somebody who was not here can act on.
- **The one unit I took**, and where it ended up: one named step off the do-this page, finished and with both owners, or a stop on a named lock.
- **What is still open**, each with its actual next step. "Follow up" is not a next step.
- **What I could not answer**, in this repo's own words: not recorded.

Two things make that note worth reading. **Never invent a name, an owner, a date, a figure, or a link to make a line look finished** — an unknown left as an unknown is the useful answer, and a plausible filler reads to the next person as something you checked. And **never quietly drop an item the last note left open**: if it closed, say it closed; if it did not, carry it forward.

That second rule bites in a particular way here, because the open items on this seat are absences that repetition makes feel resolved. **An absent step-timing record does not age into an existing one** — the more passes that run without one, the more obviously a plausible percentage would fit, and that is the pressure rather than the permission. **A seat with nobody recorded in it does not become filled by habit**, however many passes have been run from it. **Hoffman being a question does not age into being a Current client**, and **0 Current clients on an extract does not age into a live campaign** because time has passed since the extract. Carry each one forward in the same words until somebody actually changes it.

Two things have to be in the note explicitly, because both are easy to leave implied. **Name the step you took, off the page, in the page's own words** — a note reading "moved the campaign forward" tells the next person nothing about which step ran, or whether it was on the page at all. And **say that it went to both owners.** A note that names one reads like a note where the handoff happened, and the other owner never hears about it.

No stamps, no status tags, no run identifiers. A person reads this.
